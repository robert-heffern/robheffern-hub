Capitals

A small project about which letters we choose to make big.

Every letter you know comes in two sizes. A and a are the same letter wearing different clothes, and somewhere in your head is a rule that turns one into the other. I tried to teach that rule to a small neural network — not as a lookup table of "A goes with a," but as a transformation you could point at any shape. Then I pointed it at shapes that have no capital at all.

It learned the shape of "make it bigger"

Same letter, both cases — produced by the model, not looked up. The striking part: it works in alphabets you may not be able to read. Nobody told it these were related; it generalized the idea of casing across scripts.

So what's the capital of a 3?

Scripts with no capitals

Most of the world's writing systems never invented a capital/small distinction at all. Hand the model Japanese, Hebrew, Arabic, or Devanagari and ask for the "uppercase," and it does the only honest thing: almost nothing. There's no bigger version to reach for, so it hands the glyph back.

The two things everyone draws

Let's be adults about this. You put a drawing box on the open internet, and within about four seconds someone draws a swastika, and within five, a penis. It is as reliable as gravity.

So here is the section about them. There are no pictures in it. Not out of primness — the model would earnestly hunt for the capital of either one, and the result is honestly kind of funny — but because this page runs in your browser and shows only what you draw. Nothing you make here is uploaded, saved, or shown to anyone else, which means the one person who has to look at your swastika is you. Draw whatever you like. I just don't have to host it.

The grown-up version of the point: there's no server in the loop, so there's nothing to moderate and nothing to leak. The box is a mirror, not a megaphone.

How it works

The part for the engineers in the room.

The data makes itself. Instead of collecting hand-drawn letters, I render both cases of every letter from thousands of fonts. Each font gives a perfectly matched, same-style pair — and in the same hand — across every alphabet that has upper- and lowercase, all at once. Unicode's own case tables supply the ground truth, so the pairing is never guessed. That's ~94,000 clean pairs over 431 fonts and twelve scripts, generated in minutes.

It's a hybrid. A conditional U-Net does the real work: image in, a direction flag (make-it-upper / make-it-lower), image out. Because it learns casing as an operation on pixels rather than a dictionary of letters, it applies to inputs it was never trained on — which is the whole reason a 3 produces anything at all. A separate classifier recognizes known letters and reports its confidence, so the system can tell the difference between "I know this letter" and "I'm improvising."

Honest testing. The validation set is split by font family, not by sample — the model is scored only on typefaces it never saw during training. Good scores there mean it learned casing, not that it memorized specific glyphs.

It runs on your phone. Trained on a laptop GPU, exported to ONNX, quantized to a 5 MB file, and executed in your browser with onnxruntime-web. No server does any of the thinking — which is also why nothing you draw ever leaves your device.
