Alphabet Weather

A weather map of letterspace — where "next" blows, stalls, and spins.

Every glyph a small AI knows sits somewhere on this map, and at each one there's an arrow: the direction "the next letter" would push it. Read it like a weather chart. The wind is the one thing you really need — follow the arrow off any letter to see where succession carries it. Turn the other layers on only when you're curious: height is the best guess at an "uphill" for the whole field (it mostly fails — that's the interesting part), and storms marks where the wind spins instead of flowing, the places the alphabet curls back on itself.

Positions are a flattened shadow of a 64-dimensional space, so distances are only roughly meaningful — but the neighbourhoods and the swirl are real. Drag nothing; just toggle.

What to look for

With just wind on, pick a script and trace its alphabet — the arrows don't march in one tidy direction, they wheel around. Switch on storms and you'll see why: bright patches are where the wind rotates, and there are a lot of them. Those are the places where "A comes before B comes before C" quietly curves back on itself. Switch on height and watch the arrows ignore it — if the alphabet rolled downhill, every arrow would run from red to blue. They don't. That disagreement, across every script at once, is the whole finding: the alphabet isn't a staircase you descend. It's a weather system.

The map here is built on the space from Letterspace; the still blobs it drifts into are the subject of Fixed Points.
